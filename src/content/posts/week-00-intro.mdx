This is a weekly comic-book argument about quantum mechanics.

Here's the format: every issue takes one thing a comic book, or a movie, or
a superhero's internal monologue, confidently gets wrong about quantum
physics — and takes it apart. Not to dunk on the comic. Most of these
claims are wrong in genuinely interesting ways, the way a good lie tells
you something true about what people wish were real. Doctor Manhattan
thinks he's outside of time. He isn't, but the reason he isn't is more
interesting than the claim was.

## Why comics, specifically

Because they already committed to an answer. A textbook hedges. A comic
book draws Doctor Manhattan's blue hand pointing at a photon and says
*this is what's really happening in there*, in a caption box, in bold.
That's a testable claim. Most physics popularization spends its time
building up to a idea; this project gets to start by knocking one down,
which turns out to be a faster way to see what's actually being claimed.

## What you actually get each issue

Prose, and then something you can break.

Every real issue — starting with [Issue 01](/posts/week-01-superposition) —
ships with a small interactive simulation built for that week's concept
specifically, not a generic widget. You'll usually be asked to predict
what happens before you're allowed to touch the controls, because the
gap between what you predicted and what the sim actually does is where
the concept lives. Where it makes sense, there's also a forbidden
operation — a button that does the thing quantum mechanics won't let you
do, wired up to actually let you press it and watch it fail.

## How to read it

In order, ideally — later issues build on earlier ones, and each post
tells you up front what it assumes you already have. But nothing here
is gated. If you already know what superposition is and just want to
watch [the amplitude dial](/sims/amplitude-dial) do its thing, go ahead.

That's it. [Issue 01](/posts/week-01-superposition) is next, and it's
about the difference between "we don't know which" and "there isn't a
which yet" — which Doctor Manhattan gets backwards in a way that's worth
your time.
